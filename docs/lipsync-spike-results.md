# Lipsync spike results — PT-BR blind comparison (P2 Task 7)

- Date scored: 2026-08-18 (videos rendered 2026-08-14 and 2026-08-18)
- Decision: recorded in [ADR 0007](adr/0007-lipsync-engine.md)
- Scorer: Daniel Nichiata. Method: systematic frame-by-frame review of the
  60 fps renders — each video segmented at mouth changes, each shape matched
  numerically against reference renders of the nine mouths (generated by
  `lipsync preview` itself, ≥10× margin between best and second match), and
  the audio's target instants timed independently from the WAV (envelope,
  spectral bands, spectrogram). All 24 scores were locked before any
  `key.json` was opened and before any `*.viseme.json` was read.

## What was compared

Two automatic viseme-track generators, rendered through the identical
pipeline (same nine-mouth sheet, same preview document, same renderer),
muxed with the source audio, blinded as `left`/`right` by the compare
command's hidden name-hash assignment:

- **Arm "Rhubarb"** — Rhubarb 1.14.0 in language-independent phonetic mode
  (`-r phonetic`, purely acoustic; never sees the transcript).
- **Arm "WhisperX"** — WhisperX 3.8.6 forced alignment of the committed
  transcript, fed through our frozen grapheme→viseme map (PT rules differ
  from EN; see `packages/e2e/lipsync/README.md`).

Fixtures: the committed human recordings `pt-br-01.wav` (the gate, 9.64 s)
and `en-01.wav` (the control, 9.36 s), one take each, matched pair (RMS
0.08 dB apart). Rubric: four axes, 1–5 each — closure on bilabials (p/b/m),
rounding on o/u, jitter, drift over the full clip. Anchors: 3 = usable
(hits most target words, misses don't distract); a score line "reaches 3"
only if **every axis** is ≥3 (the gate owner's ruling on the plan's
"reaches 3/5" wording — minimum axis, not mean).

## Round 1 (2026-08-14) — PT round compromised, EN valid

Scored blind on 2026-08-18. During scoring, audio analysis surfaced that
**the PT take does not contain "passo a passo,"** — the transcript carried
it, the delivery skipped it. Verified independently by silence map (one
1.14 s speech block where the transcript demands ~13 syllables plus a comma
pause), spectrogram (a single connected phrase, then an unvoiced breath),
and git chronology (the phrase predates the recording — a reading slip).
Forced alignment placed the three ghost words into the pause/breath, so the
round is biased against the transcript-consuming arm exactly as the fixture
README warns. The PT-1 scores stand as historical record, not gate
evidence. The EN control matches its transcript 22/22 and stands.

## Round 2 (2026-08-18) — the PT round that counts

The transcript was corrected to the 22 words the take actually says, the
WhisperX arm regenerated (22/22 words placed, largest inter-word gap
0.74 s), the Rhubarb arm reused unchanged (it is acoustic and never saw the
transcript), and a fresh blinded compare rendered under new file names
(fresh assignment draw). Frame-collapse zero in both arms; both sides
9.64 s.

Integrity of the re-run, verified after unblinding:

- Rhubarb side is **bit-identical** to round 1 (frame-hash equal); the new
  draw landed it on the same side by coincidence.
- The WhisperX track lost exactly 10 cues, all in 3.37–4.33 s — the region
  where round 1's alignment squeezed "binária" to make room for the ghost
  words. Nothing was added elsewhere.

PT-BR round 2 (left = Rhubarb, right = WhisperX):

| Axis            | Rhubarb (left) | WhisperX (right) |
| --------------- | -------------- | ---------------- |
| p/b/m closure   | 3              | 2                |
| o/u rounding    | 2              | 3                |
| Jitter          | 4              | 2                |
| Drift           | 5              | 4                |

Identical to round 1, and not by inertia: the corrected window swapped one
miss for another inside 3.28–4.67 s (the "binária" b lost its accidental
cover; the "á" gained a well-timed open mouth; the word's tail got
swallowed by an early rest), and no axis crossed a rubric step. The 12-shape
churn of round 1 became three held shapes. **The difference between the
arms on this take never came from the ghost words — it is each arm's base
behavior.**

## Per-engine findings (attributed after unblinding)

Both arms carry the same signature in both languages — the blind
cross-language fingerprints matched the keys exactly.

**Rhubarb (phonetic mode)** — precise pressed closures, no rest, weak
rounding. When it closes, it is exact to the frame (PT: pessoal, exemplo,
compara-m, metade; EN: morning, frame-m, from, puppet-pp). But it misses
other bilabials outright (PT: Bom, vamos, busca, binária; EN: move-m, by,
pose-p, back-b), spends rounded vowels in teeth shapes (PT "Hoje"/"busca",
EN "move"), and never rests in X — it holds the pressed A through the
mid-clip breath. Very stable (few 1-frame shapes) and zero drift.

**WhisperX + grapheme map** — perfect rests, no pressed closure, jittery.
Every pause lands on a relaxed X exactly; but bilabial closures come out as
relaxed X or open shapes rather than the pressed A (it never produced A in
any clip), short words strobe (7–8 one-frame shapes; an E-B-C-G-D-X strobe
across "Hoje/vamos"), and a slight delay accumulates late in the clip
(~+0.05–0.08 s). Word-level *timing* from forced alignment is otherwise
solid in both languages.

The failures are complementary (each arm is strong exactly where the other
is weak) and neither is language-specific: the EN control shows the same
signatures and also has no all-axes-≥3 side.

## Outcome

No PT-BR side reaches ≥3 on every axis — Rhubarb fails rounding (2),
WhisperX fails closure (2) and jitter (2). Per the plan's decision rule,
the P2 gate is **at risk**; the decision and its consequences are ADR 0007.

## North-star English control (2026-08-19; diagnostic only)

At Daniel's request, the 90 s north-star was translated and synthesized with
an English voice to isolate language/voice from the visual document. Edge TTS
`en-US-AvaNeural` read the exact 250-word English transcript; local WhisperX
alignment placed 250/250 tokens.

The untouched English adapter track reached A on all 59 aligned `p`, `b`, and
`m` characters, compared with 87/108 on the matched PT-BR north-star draft.
Closure is therefore materially better in this control. Jitter is not: 632 of
889 English cue intervals were shorter than 0.100 s (the PT draft had 595 of
957). Applying the same three-frame minimum reduced English to 403 cues while
preserving 59/59 closures and 63 measured rests.

This run was not blind, used synthetic voices, and does not revise ADR 0007 or
replace the PT-BR gate. It narrows the re-spike: the English grapheme path can
place closures, but a minimum-hold/timing layer remains necessary independent
of language.

## Honesty about blindness

Round 2 was key-sealed but not cognitively naive: the scorer already knew
both arms' signatures from round 1, and frame-identity between rounds made
the reused arm detectable regardless of the fresh assignment draw. What
keeps the scores meaningful is that they anchor to objective per-word
checks (did the mouth reach the pressed shape at the measured consonant
instant), which do not depend on knowing which arm is which — and the
blind fingerprint predictions matching the keys afterwards is evidence the
method measured the videos, not expectations.
